Privacy notice

Kimbia Sync · Updated September 2026

Kimbia Sync is a small, open-source iOS app. It has no server, no analytics and no accounts. The developer collects no data about you.

What the app reads

With your permission, the app reads your workouts from Apple Health: the kind of activity, when it started and ended, and the distance, energy, heart rate, elevation and route recorded with it. It never writes to Apple Health.

On your device

The app stores your AT Protocol tokens, and the DPoP key that protects them, in the iOS Keychain. They stay on this device: they are not included in backups and do not move to a new phone. The tokens go only to your own PDS and its authorisation server.

The app also keeps a list of the workouts it has synced, and your choice of activity types, in its private storage. Deleting the app deletes these.

Signing out asks your authorisation server to revoke the tokens, then deletes them from the device. iOS keeps Keychain items after an app is removed, where no other app can read them; if you install the app again, it deletes them the first time it opens.

Who the app contacts

The app connects to these services and to no others.

  • Your PDS and its authorisation server, to sign in, to keep your sign-in fresh, and to write and delete your activity records. Your account decides which servers these are.
  • plc.directory and public.api.bsky.app, only when you sign in, to turn your handle into the address of your PDS. (An account with a did:web identity is looked up on the site that identity names instead.) If that fails, the app asks the website your handle is named after. These look-ups are public and use no credentials.

What you publish

The workouts you choose to sync are written as app.kimbia.activity records in your AT Protocol repository. Records in an AT Protocol repository are public. The app only asks for permission to write Kimbia's activity records.

Each record has the kind of activity, its distance, moving time, average speed, elevation gain and the device that recorded it. By default it shows only the day of the activity, not the time, and its map leaves out the first and last 500 metres so that it does not show where you start or finish. In Settings you can share exact times and the full route, or hide the map.

What the app does not collect

  • No analytics, no crash reports and no telemetry.
  • No advertising identifiers.
  • The app sends nothing to the developer.
